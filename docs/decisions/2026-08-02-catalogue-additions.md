# Catalogue additions: what went in, what is blocked, and one thing we had wrong

**Status:** four added. Four verified and blocked on a taxonomy decision. Three verified and
recommended against, with reasons. One prior rejection refuted.

Three parallel sweeps checked candidates against the catalogue's four tests: a coherent action
program, an external and non-captive verdict, a route for an unaffiliated child, and a later
admissions-legible distinction.

---

## 1. Added: the county-fair mechanism, through the door we were not using

The sweep was asked to extend 4-H beyond woodworking and growing plants. It found that **4-H is the
weaker half of the mechanism**, and the discovery is worth more than the four entries.

Almost every county fair runs an **open class** beside its 4-H division. For an unaffiliated child it
is better on every count:

| | 4-H | Open class |
|---|---|---|
| Membership | Required | None |
| County residency | Required | None — "Exhibitors do not need to be Larimer County residents to enter" |
| Age floor | 8, verified | None published |
| Youngest judged class | Junior, 8-10 | "Children, 4 and under, any craft" |
| Competitive | Yes | Yes — premiums, Champion and Reserve Champion |

That last row is the point. Open-class youth classes are competitively placed and carry premium
money, so the verdict is real. **This is what restores making activities to the bottom of the band**,
where 4-H's Cloverbud rule blocks them: Cloverbuds "do not participate in competitive events,
contests, shows… and are not eligible to receive premiums," and receive a participation ribbon
instead of a judgement.

Added: `baking`, `food-preservation`, `knitting` through open class; `pottery` through 4-H.

**Pottery stays with 4-H deliberately.** Open class has no ceramics department — clay appears only
inside a Creative Crafts class for an "item of paper, clay, stone, or any other medium not listed,"
judged on "workmanship, beauty of design, general appearance, creativity." That names nothing about
clay: no wall thickness, no glaze fault, no firing. The 4-H score sheets are genuinely
criterion-referenced, and the price of that door is the age-8 floor.

**Each entry names a specific fair.** Open-class rules vary in both directions — Erie County's Needle
Arts competition is "Anyone 13 years old and older," which would exclude most of our band. The
mechanism is general; the floor is not.

### Rejected within this cluster

- **"Fibre craft" as one entry.** Knitting is two needles, crochet is one hook, weaving is a loom.
  No shared motor sequence — the "crafts" failure mode one level down. The fair books refuse to
  bundle them too, listing crochet, hand knit and felted as separate judged classes.
- **Weaving,** on cost. A floor loom is $200 to $1,500 before yarn, and there is no ceiling above the
  county ribbon to justify it.
- **Cake decorating,** as part of baking. Piping and fondant share no motor sequence with mixing and
  proving. It is a defensible separate entry, not part of this one.

## 2. RULED OUT: sport, on scope rather than on evidence

**Decided 2026-08-02 by the owner. This is a scope boundary, not a finding, and it should not be
re-litigated by a future sweep that notices the athletic channel is empty.**

Sport is out of scope for this catalogue. Not because these pursuits fail the four tests — three of
them pass, and track and field passes better than most of what is already in — but because the
admissions route they lead to is not the one this product exists to open.

Two reasons, and the second is the load-bearing one:

1. It is already well understood that children reach selective universities through sport. It is not
   a route anybody needs us to discover for them.
2. **It needs a world-class coach, and the whole premise here is the child who has not got one.**
   The catalogue's third test asks whether an unaffiliated child can take part. Athletics clears that
   test at the *entry* gate — USATF unattached status is real and it runs to Nationals — but clears
   nothing at the *training* gate. A child who can enter a meet without a club still cannot become
   competitive without coaching, facilities and a squad. That is the same distinction that removed
   judo below, and it applies across sport.

So the athletic gap in the catalogue is deliberate, and the note below records what was verified in
case the boundary is ever revisited.

### What was verified before the boundary was drawn



`track-and-field`, `orienteering` and `sport-stacking` all pass cleanly, and track and field passes
better than most of what is already in the catalogue. USATF Rule 306.1(g): *"An athlete may withdraw
their club affiliation and compete unattached"* — unattached is a first-class category all the way to
the National Championship. Orienteering's rules admit any age to a national championship class, and
its Junior National Championships name homeschooling in eligibility, which nothing else we checked
does. Sport stacking bars a family member from judging a final, by rule.

There was also a mechanical blocker, now moot: `CABINS` is a closed eight-element array with no
athletic cabin, and `art-motion` is visual arts despite the name. Adding one would have touched the
inference engine's coverage maths and the browse wall's grouping. With sport out of scope the
taxonomy stays as it is, which is the cheaper answer to the same question.

`juggling` is verified but recommended against on its merits: its published standard rewards "a stage
performed juggling act that is entertaining and marketable to a broad audience," which is an
arts-performance rubric rather than an athletic one, so it does not fill the hole it appears to. And
reaching the finals means $400 of festival registration plus travel to Indiana.

## 3. Blocked: drama and speech have a venue but not a home

The graded-exam mechanism transfers cleanly. LAMDA is structurally **better** than ABRSM on the
unaffiliated-child test: *"There are no minimum age restrictions"* in the syllabus, and *"Your teacher
may arrange for you to take an exam, but you can also book one yourself."* Ofqual regulation makes the
examiner external by rule, so it cannot be the child's own teacher.

- `acting` — ceiling is the **ESU National Shakespeare Competition**, and it has the only clean
  precedent in this whole round: Delilah Delgado, 2021 national winner, started at Williams that
  autumn.
- `speaking-in-public` — a genuinely distinct action program from `debate`: the child writes their own
  material, there is no opponent and no assigned side.
- `reciting` — ceiling is Poetry Out Loud, which names homeschoolers in its rules.

`speaking-in-public` fits `influence-media/rhetoric`. `acting` and `reciting` do not have an obvious
home: `art-motion` is visual, and filing performance under `influence-media/storytelling` would put
acting in the same belief as marketing. Same decision as the athletic cabin, and worth taking
together.

**Region is the real weakness here, not age or affiliation.** Both boards are UK-first, so
`reach: alone` is honest for a British child and optimistic for an American one.

## 4. Corrected: public speaking was cut on a fact that is wrong

The catalogue header said public speaking "needs a chartered Gavel Club that an individual child
cannot start." Toastmasters' Gavel Clubs do work that way, but they are not the only door, and LAMDA's
ladder is regulated, externally examined and bookable by a parent. The header now says so.

## 5. Refuted: martial arts

We rejected martial arts on the grounds that the judge is captive — belt tests are administered by
the child's own instructor. **That is true and too narrow.** Belt-testing is not the sport's
competitive venue. USA Judo's 2026 Junior Olympic entry packet states: *"Club affiliation is not
required to compete in this tournament."* Only individual membership is needed, there is a Novice
category for white, yellow and orange belts, referees are neutral IJF-certified officials, and USA
Judo publishes cumulative U13 and U15 national ranking lists.

Still not added, but **for a different and honest reason**: judo cannot be practised alone. Randori
needs a partner and a mat, so an unaffiliated child can enter the tournament but cannot arrive ready.
That is `needs-organisation`, and it fails the training half of reach rather than the entry half.

The distinction matters because the old reason was checkable and wrong.

## Rejections confirmed, with the quotes

- **Gymnastics.** *"Unaffiliated athletes will NOT be permitted to participate in any USA Gymnastics
  sanctioned event."* Categorical.
- **Swimming**, with a correction to our reasoning. The rulebook is *permissive* — unattached status
  cannot be used to bar a swimmer, and unattached swimmers are eligible for National Age Group
  records. But it delegates: *"a host club or LSC may require unattached swimmers to arrange for their
  own coach supervision,"* and the parent cannot be that coach. Access depends on a stranger's
  signature on the day, which is arguably worse than a closed door because it is unpredictable.
- **Cambridge English Young Learners.** No verdict is possible: *"There is no pass or fail result."*
  Every child gets shields. Same fault as Cloverbuds and Make It With Wool's Young Sewers division.
- **Royal Academy of Dance.** *"Only RAD Registered Teachers are able to enter students for their
  exams."* Everything else passes, which makes it the cleanest illustration of the unaffiliated-child
  test doing the work alone.

## Dead venues, and one that is not

- **Siemens Competition** — dead. Final competition 2017, announced February 2018.
- **Google Science Fair** — dead. Ran 2011-2018; the domain returns 404.
- **AMNH Young Naturalist Awards** — dead. The museum writes it in the past tense: *"From 2003-2016…"*
- **Zaner-Bloser National Handwriting Contest** — **paused, not dead**, and we had this wrong. The site
  carries a dated notice: it is not accepting entries for 2025-26 *"as we take time to consider the
  contest's future."* It renders no verdict this cycle, so it fails the venue test now, but it ran
  normally through 2025 and is explicitly under review. Re-check next year.
- **National Festival of Breads youth division** — removed, and it is the loss that empties baking's
  ceiling. 2017: *"open to any AMATEUR YOUTH baker 8 to 17 years of age."* 2025: *"open to ADULT home
  bakers, 18 years of age and older."*

## A test that had to change

Two assertions broke on the four additions, both proportional, both the same fault the age-floor
assertions had. `ceiling === undefined` was bounded at 10% of the catalogue, so adding three entries
that honestly have no ceiling turned the suite red and the cheapest way to green would have been to
invent ceilings — which, unlike a wrong age floor, is a fabricated claim to an admissions reader. It
now requires that a missing ceiling be *explained in the note*, which is the actual requirement:
baking's is missing because a venue deleted its youth division, and that is worth knowing.

The regional assertion required each region to hold 60% of the catalogue, which punishes growth: four
US-only county-fair entries remove nothing a British child could do. It now asserts an absolute floor
and a spread across cabins.
