The arithmetic

α₀ = 1 + .5·env + .5·mastery + .5·disc
β₀ = 1  // nothing tilts against
r = 0.5 ^ ( days ÷ 14 )
α = α₀ + Σ ( w · role · r )
β = β₀ + Σ ( w · r ÷ max(1, set) )
n = α + β
mean = α ÷ n
var = αβ ÷ ( n·n·(n+1) )
LB = mean − √var  // the output

The lower bound, not the mean. Two events and twenty events can share a mean of 0.85. Subtracting a standard deviation separates them, so thin evidence is penalised automatically.

Two masses. α and β decay and answer what do we believe now. observedMass sums the same weights undecayed and answers have we seen enough. Observing does not un-happen.

Rows three and four both beat the 0.6 threshold and are still refused. A high number alone is never enough.

One choice moment costs one skip. The divisor is the count of cells offered, not taken and past novelty, so the six things passed over to pick a seventh share a single skip's worth between them. Without it, breadth of discovery would bury the spike: those same ten skips would drive the second row from 0.62 down to 0.38.
