Forty-four things a person can get good at.

One screen. No categories to walk through, no next page, nothing behind a menu.

Let them look for two seconds before speaking. Why flat and not a hierarchy: the closest study we found ran 36 children aged 6 to 11 against a 14-category hierarchy versus 44 flat concrete categories. Flat won at every grade, and of twelve first-graders, zero found the "More Choices" control on their own. So there is no pager here, ever. Whatever is on this screen is, for a small child, the whole world. Every tile is one render from one scaffold, normalised to the same mean brightness, because a wall that reads a click as interest cannot afford a tile that is merely prettier than its neighbours. Age control drops the roster. Nothing is locked, but a nine-year-old is not shown a thing needing a licence.

Most of what you could measure is noise.

first clickScores zero. Novelty is cheap.
time on taskScores zero. Absorbed and stuck look identical.
same-day reopenScores zero. It has not survived a night away.
prompted returnScores zero. We nudged them there.
how good the work isScores zero for interest. That is a different question.
return, later dayScores 1.0. Nobody asked them to come back.

Dwell time is still recorded and shown to a guide. It just never moves the number.

This slide is the credibility slide. Anyone can build a dashboard that counts minutes. Why time is out: dwell is non-monotonic in interest. Stronger performers stay as progress rises, weaker ones stay on the most familiar option. Same number, opposite meaning. There is a study on four-year-olds where the sign of the correlation flips with performance. There is also a mechanical trap. A return is worth 1.0. Multiply by minutes and a 40-minute session outweighs 40 returns, so duration silently becomes the dominant term. We deleted a field that would have allowed exactly that. Direct evidence: at ages 7 to 8, in-session engagement failed to separate an intrinsically motivating task from a boring one. Only a delayed test did (Habgood and Ainsworth, 2011). If a manager asks "why not track active time using clicks": that separates active from idle, which is real, but it does not separate absorbed from struggling, which is the part that breaks the inference.
Act I · find

How the number is actually made.

Each subject and way-of-working pair carries evidence for and evidence against. Nothing else.

EventWeight
Came back on a later day, unprompted+1.0
Redid it unasked · chose harder · recovered from failing · set own scope+0.5
An adult saw them into the topic itself+0.25
Passed it over, having tried it before−0.5
Passed it over, never having tried it−0.15
score = for / (for + against) then subtract one standard deviation
every event halves in weight after 14 days
skips divide by how many options were on screen

These are priors, not fitted parameters. The first real cohort recalibrates them.

Read the table, do not read the formula aloud. Point at it. Starting point is 1 for and 1 against, a coin flip, nudged slightly by whether the subject is in the child's environment and how they already do in it at school. We report the bottom of the uncertainty band rather than the middle, so the system under-claims. Skips are divided by the choice-set size because picking one gadget out of seven is one decision, not six rejections. Without that, breadth of discovery would suppress the very spike we are hunting. The adult report is capped at 0.25 and deliberately cannot satisfy the day gate on its own, so no number of parent reports can make a cell confident without the child having done something.

What it takes before we call it a spike.

6 units of evidenceCounted without decay. Observing does not un-happen.

six clean returns, no skips → score 0.75 · candidate

You can bank at most one return per day. Six units means roughly a week of coming back.

The worked pair at the bottom is the thing to say out loud. Same child, same returns, and the only difference is that they kept walking past it when it was on screen. That flips the verdict. At most one return per day is a real constraint, not a policy. A second visit the same day has a predecessor on the same day, so it classifies as same-day and scores nothing. If asked why 0.6: a cell with no evidence sits at 0.5, a coin flip. 0.6 means meaningfully better than knowing nothing, and it has to clear that after subtracting a standard deviation.

A visible return metric would destroy the only unprompted signal we have.

The pull quote is the strongest idea in the deck and it needs no citation, because it is an argument about our own design. Once a child knows a parent can see whether they came back, coming back becomes evidence of compliance. We would spend our one valid instrument to build a feature that also harms the child. The tempting middle path fails too. "Show parents only the good news" is conditional positive regard, which tracks introjected regulation more strongly than conditional negative regard does. Butler, 1988, is the cleanest evidence and it is in our exact age band. Fifth and sixth graders given grades, comments, or both. Interest was highest after comments, and grades plus comments performed like grades alone. The number does not sit politely beside the comment. It absorbs it.

Prove · evidencegraph

A finished artifact cannot tell you who made it.

An essay, a painting, a repo. The result is silent on whether it was the child, an AI, a parent, a tutor, or paid help.

AI detectors flagged non-native English writers at 61%. We never run one against a person. Liang et al. · Patterns · 2023
14 detectors across 754 cases were neither accurate nor reliable. None exceeded 80%. Weber-Wulff et al. · 2023

Version history is not the answer either. Timestamps are typed in and can be rewritten. That is a claim, not evidence.

Two dead-end reflexes. Detect the person: fails, and punishes the wrong children. Vanderbilt disabled Turnitin's detector citing exactly this bias. Trust the version history: it is rewritable, so it is a claim. The problem is also broader than AI. Parent, tutor and paid help all count, and no detector has ever claimed to catch those.

Record the making, not the result.

Every step content-addressed, so its fingerprint is its content. Change a byte and the id changes.

Play the story if the live version is up. Twelve steps: the plan, asking a tutor, a failed run, the collision fix, a reflection, a mentor review, and a human grade at the end. Declared help is neutral. The tutor call is right there in the record and nobody is penalised for it. Only hidden help, passed off as solo, is the failure. That is what makes it fair and hard to game. Read the last caption out loud: the final grade was decided by a person, not a machine.

Then try to break it.

The whole graph rolls up to one root a verifier recomputes independently.

A button in the product tampers with a saved step. The check fails, the root mismatches, and the graph visibly fractures. Undo restores it.

Change one saved item and the check fails. That is how you know nothing has been edited since.

Do the tamper flip live if there is time. It is the most convincing ten seconds available. Mechanism for Q and A: each node id is a SHA-256 digest of its own bytes, and the graph rolls up to an RFC-6962 Merkle root ordered by timestamp then id, so it reproduces exactly. Be precise about the claim. This proves nothing has been edited since it was written. It does not prove the steps were real, which is what the in-person defense is for. Immutability is not a legal defence for refusing to delete, and we have not solved that yet. Today the graph lives in our own database and erasing a project deletes its rows outright. The hard problem arrives when we anchor to a log we do not control.

Put this slide in deliberately. A deck arguing that provenance must be honest cannot overstate its own, and this audience will respect the disclosure far more than they would punish it. EDPB Guidelines 02/2025, adopted July 2026: technical immutability cannot be invoked to justify non-compliance with the right to erasure, and hashing is not anonymising. NIST 800-88 says crypto-erase counts only if every copy of the key dies. Other pre-live gates if asked: the ingest route has no auth or rate limiting, and consent is guide-asserted rather than verified. Nothing here has been in front of a real child.

Backup · q&a

Why not just track time?

Dwell is non-monotonic in interest. Stronger performers stay as progress rises, weaker ones stay with the most familiar option. Same number, opposite meaning.

Multiply weight by minutes and a 40-minute session outweighs 40 returns, so duration becomes the dominant term in the model.

Click-based idle detection separates active from idle. It does not separate absorbed from struggling, which is the part that breaks the inference.

The depth families are the within-day discriminator that time was supposed to be. A child who redoes something unasked gives a stronger and far more interpretable signal than one who sat there twenty minutes. We record dwell in four buckets and show it to a guide. It just never votes.
Backup · q&a

Architecture.

A pure engine holds content-addressing, Merkle roots and human-authority validation, behind storage ports, with a read-only viewer that never recomputes trust.

Any human judgment requires a named human actor. A model actor may only appear on declared assistance or review.

The engine owns every byte that gets hashed. The UI only displays roots and proofs, so it cannot fake a verdict. PassionLab hands projects over the same HTTP API a connector would use.
