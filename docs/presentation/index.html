Depth · why not time

Why we throw away the minutes.

Dwell is non-monotonic in interest. Stronger performers stay as progress rises, weaker ones stay with the most familiar option. Same number, opposite meaning.

Multiply weight by minutes and a 40-minute session outweighs 40 returns, so duration silently becomes the dominant term.

At ages 7 to 8, in-session engagement failed to separate an intrinsically motivating task from a boring one. Only a delayed test did. Click-based idle detection separates active from idle, which is real, but not absorbed from struggling, which is the part that breaks it.

There is a study on four-year-olds where the sign of the dwell correlation flips with performance. The delayed-test finding is Habgood and Ainsworth 2011, and it is in our exact age band. We deleted a duration field that would have allowed the multiplication, on purpose. The depth families on the next slide are the within-day discriminator that time was supposed to be.

Depth · the full model

Beta-Bernoulli, one cell at a time.

// a cell is one (domain × mode) pair, e.g. Music & Sound × Production
α = 1 + Σ (weight · r)  // for-evidence
β = 1 + Σ (weight · r)  // against-evidence
r = 0.5 ^ (Δdays / 14)  // half-life decay, 14 days

n = α + β
mean = α / n
var = (α · β) / (n · n · (n + 1))
lowerBound = mean − 1.0 · √var  we report the pessimistic edge

Priors start at α = β = 1, a coin flip, nudged by whether the subject exists in the child's environment and how they already do in it. Skips divide by the choice-set size, because picking one gadget out of seven is one decision, not six rejections.

If someone asks why subtract one standard deviation rather than a proper credible interval: it is a deliberately conservative point estimate that penalises thin evidence, and it is cheap to compute per cell per request. A real Beta quantile is the obvious upgrade. If someone asks where the constants came from, answer honestly. They are hand-set priors chosen from the literature, not fitted parameters. Nobody has calibrated them against a real cohort, because no real child has used this. The first cohort recalibrates them. Say that before they push.
Depth · the gates

Four things have to be true at once.

mass ≥ 6Counted without decay. Observing does not un-happen.
days ≥ 2A count can be run up in one sitting. A calendar cannot.

six clean returns → lowerBound 0.70 · candidate
the same six, all on one day → 0.76 · fails the day gate
three returns, three days → 0.62 · fails the mass gate

Figures computed by the engine. Full derivation, every definition and all twenty constants: prediction-model.html, beside this deck.

The worked pair is the thing to say out loud. Same child, same returns. The only difference is that he kept walking past it when it was on screen, and that flips the verdict. One per day is mechanical, not policy. A second visit the same day has a same-day predecessor, so it classifies as same-day and scores nothing.

A visible return metric would destroy the only unprompted signal we have.

This is the strongest argument in the deck and it needs no citation, because it is about our own design. Once Ari knows his mother can see whether he came back, coming back becomes evidence of compliance. We would spend our one valid instrument to build a feature that also harms him. The tempting middle path fails too. Show parents only the good news is conditional positive regard, which tracks introjected regulation more strongly than the negative kind. Butler 1988 is the cleanest evidence and it is in our exact age band. Fifth and sixth graders given grades, comments, or both. Interest was highest after comments, and grades plus comments performed like grades alone. The number does not sit politely beside the comment, it absorbs it.
Build · week 11

Stage, mentor, audience and rest. Two days off a week, three months a year.

The brief is an offer, never an assignment. The child owns the choice, and success is described as process prose rather than a score. He logs what he tried, what happened, what he fixed. The log is append-only and it becomes the evidence record. Getting stuck is treated as the good part. The mentor relay runs warm, then technical, then domain expert, and the audience widens from himself to a real community. If he logs a finished thing with no struggle behind it, one question appears. Prove · evidencegraph

A finished artifact cannot tell you who made it.

An essay, a track, a repo. The result is silent on whether it was the child, an AI, a parent, a tutor, or paid help.

AI detectors flagged non-native English writers at 61%. We never run one against a person. Liang et al. · Patterns · 2023
14 detectors across 754 cases were neither accurate nor reliable. None exceeded 80%. Weber-Wulff et al. · 2023

Version history is not the answer either. Timestamps are typed in and rewritable. That is a claim, not evidence.

Two dead-end reflexes. Detect the person: fails, and punishes the wrong children. Vanderbilt disabled Turnitin's detector citing exactly this bias. Trust the version history: rewritable, so it is a claim. The problem is broader than AI anyway. Parent, tutor and paid help all count, and no detector has ever claimed to catch those.

Record the making, not the result.

Twelve steps: the plan, asking a tutor, a run that failed, the fix, a mentor review, and a human grade at the end.

This is the explorer's own demo record, a small game build, because that is what ships with it. Ari's project gets the identical treatment.

Play the story if the live app is up. Declared help is neutral. The tutor call is right there in the record and nobody is penalised for it. Only hidden help, passed off as solo, is the failure. That is what makes it fair and hard to game. Every step is content-addressed, so its id is a SHA-256 digest of its own bytes. Change a byte and the id changes. Read the last caption out loud: the final grade, decided by a person, not a machine.

Then try to break it.

The whole graph rolls up to one root a verifier recomputes independently.

A button in the product tampers with a saved step. The check fails, the root mismatches, and the graph visibly fractures. Undo restores it.

Change one saved item and the check fails. That is how you know nothing has been edited since.

Do the tamper flip live if there is time. It is the most convincing ten seconds available. Be precise about the claim. This proves nothing has been edited since it was written. It does not prove the steps were real, which is what the in-person defense is for.
Depth · how the proof holds

Recomputable by someone who does not trust us.

nodeId = sha256(canonical bytes of the step) // the id is the content
root = RFC-6962 Merkle tree over all nodes
ordered by (timestamp, id), so it reproduces exactly

Any human judgment needs a named human actor. A model actor may only appear on declared assistance or review, never on a grade.

The engine owns every byte that gets hashed. The viewer is read-only and never recomputes trust, so the interface cannot fake a verdict.

Ordering by timestamp then id is what makes the root reproducible rather than insertion-dependent. Scope the claim honestly: verify today means integrity of this copy. External anchoring and signing are not built, which is the next slide.
Prove · what is not built

What we have not built.

builtContent-addressed graph, RFC-6962 roots, independent re-derivation, tamper detection
stubSigning. Attestations are in-toto shaped and unsigned
stubExternal transparency log. The anchor call returns an empty proof
stubVerifiable deletion. No key lifecycle exists yet

The bigger one is not on that list. Steps are self-reported. If a child pastes someone else's work and logs it as their own, we faithfully record the lie and then prove nobody edited it.

A tamper-evident diary is not a lie detector. What closes that gap is the child talking about the work in front of a person, which is why a human grade sits at the end of every record. Separately: immutability is not a legal defence for refusing to delete, and we have not solved that either.

Put this in deliberately. A deck arguing that provenance must be honest cannot overstate its own, and this audience will respect the disclosure more than they would punish it. EDPB Guidelines 02/2025, adopted July 2026: technical immutability cannot justify non-compliance with the right to erasure, and hashing is not anonymising. NIST 800-88: crypto-erase counts only if every copy of the key dies. Other pre-live gates if asked: the ingest route has no auth or rate limiting, consent is guide-asserted, and no real child has used any of this.

Backup · adult reports

What did you see, and on which days?

An adult report only counts if it names days. It caps at a quarter of a return and it cannot make a belief confident on its own.

Submit it and the panel says Written down. Nothing else. The channel is write-only, so no adult ever learns that their report moved something.

Deliberately flat acknowledgement. If an adult could see their report move a needle, reporting would become advocacy and we would be measuring which parent is most invested. The console line on that screen is worth reading: it can see what they did, it cannot see whose idea it was or whether they feel able to stop. Those you have to ask. This is also our answer to Achenbach: cross-informant agreement between adults about the same child runs around 0.28, so an adult report is a weak prior and never a verdict.

Backup

Reward neutrality is only the discovery window.

We are not anti-reward. Rewards are the right tool in the morning, where the target behavior is defined. Pay for throughput, get throughput.

Attach one to exploration and every child explores, so the signal measures prize-chasing. For the children who already had the interest, an expected tangible reward can crowd it out.

Once a spike is certified and the ascent begins, earned progress comes back. Phase-specific, not a philosophy.

Deci, Koestner and Ryan 1999 meta-analysis: expected tangible rewards reduce free-choice intrinsic motivation, around d = -0.28 to -0.40. Have this ready for anyone who is pro-extrinsic-reward. Reconcile rather than argue.

Architecture.

A pure engine holds content-addressing, Merkle roots and human-authority validation, behind storage ports, with a read-only viewer.

Its own repo, not a module of PassionLab. Projects arrive over the same HTTP API a connector would use, so adding a connector changes who creates a node, not the model.

Rust workspace: engine, ports, store, service, plus a JS viewer. The breadth claim is not hand-waving because manual entry and future auto-emitters produce identical node shapes.
